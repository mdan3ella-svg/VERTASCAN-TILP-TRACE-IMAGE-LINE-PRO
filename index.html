Scanning engines

TILP runs a real computer-vision pipeline. Each engine below is a different way of deciding what counts as a line; everything after that — cleanup, skeletonising, contour tracing and curve fitting — is shared.

Canny

Gaussian derivative, then interpolated non-maximum suppression to thin every edge to exactly one pixel, then a hysteresis double threshold that keeps faint continuations of strong lines and drops isolated noise. The reference method, and the right default for photographs and renders.

XDoG

Extended difference-of-Gaussians. A band-pass isolates edge-scale detail and a tanh soft-threshold rolls it into an ink-like ramp. Output reads as drawn rather than detected — best on pencil, charcoal and painterly sources.

Flow DoG

Builds an edge-tangent-flow field from the structure tensor, then filters across the flow and averages along it. Broken contours knit back into continuous strokes. Slowest engine, and the most coherent on messy sources.

Adaptive

Local-mean thresholding. Made for scanned linework and documents: a page that is bright in one corner and grey in another still binarises evenly. Pair it with Flatten lighting for phone photos of paper.

Gradient

Plain Sobel or Scharr magnitude threshold. Fastest, bluntest, and useful when you want raw edge response without hysteresis logic.
